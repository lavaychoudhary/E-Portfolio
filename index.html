DEEP DIVE

Predicting Diabetes with Machine Learning

A Random Forest "Bake-Off" — 6-Version Iterative Pipeline

PythonPandasScikit-LearnMatplotlibSeaborn

Objective

To build a robust predictive model for early diabetes detection using the Pima Indians Diabetes Dataset, optimizing the model through an iterative, 6-version "Bake-off" pipeline.

The Journey: From Raw Data to Clinical Reliability

Machine learning is rarely a straight line. To find the most reliable model, I didn't just tune hyperparameters—I iterated on the data itself.

01

The Data Reality Check Handling Missing Values

A critical first step was realizing that in medical data, a "0" for Glucose or BMI is biologically impossible—it means the data is missing, not zero.

Initial Approach: Converted invalid zeros to NaN and replaced them with the dataset's median value.
Advanced Approach (V3): Upgraded to K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) imputation, allowing the algorithm to estimate missing values based on similar patient profiles.
02

Adding Feature Engineering Domain Knowledge

Instead of letting the model figure everything out from scratch, I engineered new features to give it a head start:

Glucose_Risk Binary flag identifying patients with dangerously high Glucose levels (> 140)
BMI_Category Grouping continuous BMI numbers into established medical tiers (Normal, Overweight, Obese)
03

Establishing the Baseline Versions 1 & 2

I started by training a standard Random Forest Classifier.

V1
100 trees · depth 10
85.7% accuracy · 0.947 ROC-AUC
V2
300 trees · depth 10
86.4% accuracy — diminishing returns
04

The Data-Centric Sweet Spot Version 3 — Best Model

This was the most medically viable model. By combining the KNN imputation, engineered features, and setting class_weight='balanced' to account for the dataset's natural imbalance, I created a model focused on clinical reliability.

While accuracy remained stable (86.4%), the model's Precision jumped — it became highly reliable at ensuring that when it predicted "Diabetic," it was actually correct, reducing harmful false positives.
05

Falling into the Overfitting Trap Versions 4 & 5

To push the algorithm's limits, I ran a "Hit & Trial" phase.

V4
10 trees · unrestricted depth
89.6% accuracy — but ROC-AUC dropped. Overfitting!
V5
Only 5 trees
Highly unstable predictions — too few trees
06

The Volume Check Version 6

Altered the train/test split from 80/20 to 70/30. The reduction in training data caused a dip in model confidence and accuracy, confirming that for small datasets (under 1,000 samples), maximizing training data volume is essential.

Key Takeaways

Data Beats Algorithms

The biggest leap in clinical reliability came from engineering the Glucose_Risk feature and using intelligent KNN imputation — not from tripling the number of trees.

Accuracy is a Liar

Version 4 had the highest accuracy (89.6%) but was dangerously overfit. ROC-AUC and Precision provided a much truer picture of diagnostic power.

Hyperparameter Ceilings

Once an ensemble reaches critical mass, further tuning yields minimal returns compared to cleaning and understanding the data.
